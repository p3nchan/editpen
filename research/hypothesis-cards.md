# editpen hypothesis cards v2

> Each card states a falsifiable statement, metric, kill criterion, and validation timeline.
> These cards sit below the project-level hypothesis (LLM-generated Chinese carries measurable translationese features). Each card tests one feature family that the feature map (`feature-map.md`) identifies as both measurable and practitioner-salient.
>
> **Shared analysis contract.** All five hypotheses are tested on the same planned corpus under the same preregistered protocol. Estimands are author-clustered (the unit of observation is the per-author mean, not the per-text value) to account for within-author correlation. The multiplicity policy is Holm-Bonferroni across the five primary tests at family-wise α = 0.05. Kill decisions are applied in order H1→H5 after all five adjusted p-values are computed; no card is killed or kept before all five are evaluated. If data for a card is insufficient (fewer than 10 findings per rule on the sealed evaluation set), the card is deferred, not killed.

## H1. Contrastive frame density

**Falsifiable statement.** Chinese text generated by LLMs from a Chinese prompt contains contrastive frames (「不是 X，而是 Y」and its 34 variants in the internal pilot checklist, F1) at a higher author-clustered density than matched human-authored zh-TW text in the same genre.

**Estimand.** Difference in author-level mean contrastive-frame density (instances per 1,000 characters) between LLM and human groups, on genre-matched samples ≥500 characters.

**Metric.** Contrastive-frame instances per 1,000 characters, counted by the pilot checklist F1 regex set (high-precision bucket + medium-precision bucket). Report per-author means, bootstrap 95% CI of the group difference, and the full per-author distribution.

**Kill criterion.** The adjusted (Holm-Bonferroni) 95% CI of the LLM−human author-clustered mean difference includes zero. If the CI excludes zero in the positive direction (LLM > human), the feature survives; if in the negative direction, it is contradicted.

**Validation timeline.** Measurable at corpus build with the existing regex. Tested on sealed evaluation sets.

**Evidence.** The practitioner survey ranks this as the #1 tell (18 distinct X authors, S177). The pilot checklist's F1 has been in use since 2026-04-24 but without a measured false-positive rate on untouched human text. The literature search found no corpus measurement of this specific frame family in AI-generated Chinese; the practitioner signal is strong but unmeasured. The translationese literature has no direct ancestor for it — the contrastive frame is an RLHF artifact, not a translation universal.

## H2. Conjunction and connective overuse

**Falsifiable statement.** LLM-generated Chinese uses explicit conjunctions and discourse connectives (路標詞: 首先, 其次, 最後, 此外, 總結來說, 值得注意的是, 然而, 因此, etc.) at a higher author-clustered density than human-authored Chinese of matched genre.

**Estimand.** Difference in author-level mean connective density (instances per 10,000 tokens, POS-tagged following S129/Xiao 2010 methodology) between LLM and human groups, on length- and genre-matched samples.

**Metric.** POS-tagged conjunction count per 10,000 tokens, author-clustered. Secondary: type profile (ratio of high-frequency simple connectives to low-frequency formal connectives). Genre is a covariate, not a separate gate.

**Kill criterion.** The adjusted 95% CI of the LLM−human author-clustered mean difference includes zero.

**Validation timeline.** Requires POS tagging (CKIP or jieba + custom connective lexicon). Measurable at corpus build; tested on sealed evaluation sets.

**Evidence.** S129 (Xiao 2010) reports translated Chinese has 306.42 conjunctions per 10,000 tokens vs 243.23 in original Chinese (LL=723.12, p<0.001). The practitioner survey (S177) ranks filler frames (#2, 12 authors) and 翻譯腔 (#4, 9 authors) as top tells. Pilot checklist F4 (路標詞密度 ≤3/1000 chars) and F6 (結尾升華) already lint for this, but without a measured baseline. S026 (Zhu et al. 2023 CCL, CTAP) found ChatGPT used more connectives/function words than humans.

## H3. Overt subject-pronoun frequency

**Falsifiable statement.** LLM-generated Chinese uses overt subject-position pronouns (我, 你, 他, 她, 它, 我們, 他們) at a higher author-clustered frequency than human-authored Chinese of matched genre.

**Estimand.** Difference in author-level mean overt-subject-pronoun rate (proportion of sentences containing an explicit subject pronoun in dependency-parsed subject position) between LLM and human groups.

**Metric.** Proportion of sentences with a dependency-tagged subject pronoun, author-clustered. Note: this metric measures surface frequency of overt pronouns, not the rate of pro-drop failure (which would require annotating zero-anaphora opportunities — a separate research task outside the corpus-build scope).

**Kill criterion.** The adjusted 95% CI of the LLM−human author-clustered mean difference includes zero.

**Validation timeline.** Requires dependency parsing (CKIP Transformers or LTP). Measurable at corpus build; tested on sealed evaluation sets.

**Evidence.** S132 (Wang & Hu 2010) reports higher frequencies for all pronoun categories in translated Chinese. S049 (Hu et al. 2018) shows subject-position pronouns are highly discriminative between translated and original Chinese. S137 (Yu Guangzhong) criticizes unnecessary pronoun retention as 歐化. Pilot checklist F10 addresses this but has no regex (human-check only). The practitioner survey does not rank this as a top-10 tell. S041 (Guo et al. 2025) reports English-influenced syntax in LLM Chinese, though exact pronoun counts are [number unverified].

## H4. Nominalization and weak-verb patterns

**Falsifiable statement.** LLM-generated Chinese uses verb nominalization (「進行 + abstract noun」, 「實現 + abstract noun」, 「達到 + abstract noun」) and abstract-noun packaging (「的重要性」, 「的必要性」) at a higher author-clustered frequency than human-authored Chinese of matched genre.

**Estimand.** Difference in author-level mean nominalization density (weak-verb + abstract-noun collocations per 1,000 characters) between LLM and human groups.

**Metric.** Count of weak-verb + abstract-noun collocations per 1,000 characters, using a lexicon of light verbs (進行, 實現, 達到, 形成, 作出, 予以, 加以) followed by abstract noun patterns. Author-clustered. Secondary: blinded-editor precision on a random sample of ≥30 flagged instances (do flagged instances actually sound unnatural to a native editor?).

**Kill criterion.** The adjusted 95% CI of the LLM−human author-clustered mean difference includes zero. A surviving card is further qualified: if blinded-editor precision on the flagged sample falls below 70%, the feature survives as a frequency signal but does not warrant a prescriptive rule.

**Validation timeline.** Requires a nominalization lexicon and POS-based pattern matching. Measurable at corpus build; tested on sealed evaluation sets.

**Evidence.** S137 (Yu) and S144 (闫 & 韩 2021) document nominalization as a core 歐化 feature. S144 provides annotation counts: student interpreters averaged 1.48 nominalizations per passage vs 0.35 for professionals. Pilot checklist F9 already has a regex (info level). The practitioner survey does not surface this explicitly. No source found measures nominalization specifically in LLM-generated (non-translated) Chinese.

## H5. `的` stacking and NP-modifier overuse

**Falsifiable statement.** LLM-generated Chinese inserts `的` between noun phrases and stacks nested `的` modifiers (depth ≥2: `NP1 的 NP2 的 NP3`) at a higher author-clustered frequency than human-authored Chinese of matched genre.

**Estimand.** Difference in author-level mean `的`-chain density (chains of depth ≥2 per 1,000 characters, constituency- or dependency-parsed) between LLM and human groups.

**Metric.** Count of `的`-linked NP chains (depth ≥2) per 1,000 characters, using constituency or dependency parsing. Author-clustered. Secondary: proportion of `的` tokens in all tokens.

**Kill criterion.** The adjusted 95% CI of the LLM−human author-clustered mean difference includes zero.

**Validation timeline.** Requires constituency parsing (Stanford NLP or CKIP). Measurable at corpus build; tested on sealed evaluation sets.

**Evidence.** S049 (Hu et al. 2018) reports that `NP + 的 + NP` (DNP/DEG structures) are among the most discriminative features between translated and original Chinese, with syntactic features alone reaching F1 89-94%. S137 (Yu) provides extensive examples of `的` stacking as 歐化. No pilot checklist rule currently targets `的` stacking specifically (F1–F11 do not include it). No source found measures `的` stacking in LLM-generated Chinese specifically.

## Card selection rationale

These five hypotheses are ranked by a combination of:
1. **Measurability** (can we build a detector with existing NLP tools?)
2. **Practitioner salience** (does the practitioner survey or editor tradition flag it?)
3. **Translationese foundation** (does the feature have corpus evidence in translated Chinese?)
4. **Gap** (is this untested in LLM-generated Chinese?)

H1 is the most practitioner-salient but least grounded in translationese theory (it's an RLHF artifact). H2–H5 bridge translationese and AI-Chinese. H3 and H5 have the strongest corpus evidence from translated Chinese but are untested on direct LLM generation. H4 has annotation-level evidence from interpreting studies.

All five share one analysis contract (author-clustered estimands, Holm-Bonferroni multiplicity, deterministic decision order) and are tested on the same corpus. Kill decisions are CI-based, never p-value-threshold-only.
